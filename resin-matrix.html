Resin Matrix

I print with an Anycubic resin printer, and Anycubic sells 17 resins. Before buying my next bottle I wanted to know which one was tough enough for functional parts without overpaying. The manufacturer's comparison page didn't make that easy, so I built my own: every resin, every published spec and the current price, sortable in one table.

Resin Matrix

reportThe problem

The official comparison is built for browsing, not choosing

Anycubic does publish a resin comparison page. It lists properties down the side and resins across the top, so the table runs about 4,160 pixels wide: close to three screen widths of sideways scrolling on a laptop. You can't sort it, it has no prices, and it was out of date. It still listed two resins the store no longer sells and left out two of the newest, 16K Standard and BJD Wear-Resistant.

My question was simple: which resin takes a hit best for under about $30 a kilo? Answering it on that page meant scrolling back and forth and holding numbers in my head. Answering it on each product page meant opening 17 tabs.

databaseThe data

Seventeen spec sheets, one table

I pulled every value from the Specification tab of each product page on Anycubic's US store: hardness, tensile strength, elongation, impact strength, flexural strength and modulus, Young's modulus, shrinkage, viscosity, density, exposure time, wash method, odor and the 1 kg price.

Putting the numbers side by side exposed problems you'd never notice one page at a time.

  • check_circleAnycubic swaps two labels on every page. The figure called “bending modulus” (about 15–70 MPa) is really flexural strength, and “bending strength” (350–3,300 MPa) is really the modulus. The matrix uses the correct names.
  • check_circleSome store listings disagreed with the product's own spec sheet. Where they differed, I used the spec sheet and noted it on the page.
  • check_circleEvery value is stored as a published range, like 35–45 MPa, rather than flattened to one number, so the table shows exactly what Anycubic claims.

table_rowsThe core pattern

Resins down, properties across

Flipping Anycubic's layout was the main design decision. With one resin per row, every property becomes a sortable column, and all sixteen columns fit across a large desktop screen without scrolling. Click Elongation and the most flexible resins rise to the top. Click Price and the cheapest do.

A star marks the best value in each column, whether that's the highest (impact, hardness) or the lowest (price, shrinkage). Seven leader cards above the table answer the most common questions outright, and clicking one sorts the table by that property.

Family filters for Standard, Detail, ABS-Like, Water-Wash, Tough and Specialty, plus switches for water-washable only and hiding sold-out resins, narrow 17 rows down to the handful worth comparing.

paletteLook and feel

Styled like Anycubic's own store

I wanted the matrix to feel like it belonged next to the store it draws from, so I read the store's live styles in the browser and borrowed them: the #005AFF blue, the fully rounded pill buttons, light gray panels and MiSans Latin, with Inter as the fallback the store itself uses. Light and dark modes share the same blue, and a single sun and moon button in the corner switches between them.

lightbulbExplaining the numbers

Answers in place, not in a glossary

A table full of MPa and J/m is only useful if you know what they mean for a print. Rather than adding a glossary, I put explanations where the questions come up.

  • check_circleHovering a column name explains the property in plain words and gives its unit. It also says whether higher or lower is better, or, for stiffness, that neither end is better.
  • check_circleHovering a resin's name lists what Anycubic recommends it for.
  • check_circleChoosing a family shows a short note on what that type of resin is good at and what it costs you, with numbers taken from the table.

content_cutIteration

Most of the edits were deletions

The first version said everything at once. Each resin's name carried its wash method, odor and a sentence of uses. Every value had a small bar underneath showing where its range sat against the lineup, every header had a line of units, and an intro paragraph and section headings sat above it all. I reviewed it as a buyer would and took things away.

  • check_circleThe range bars went first. They looked like data visualization but didn't change any decision I made, and they doubled the height of every cell.
  • check_circleUnits and “higher is better” notes moved from under each header into its tooltip, so every header fits on one line.
  • check_circleThe use notes under each name became a hover tooltip, and wash method and odor got their own sortable columns.
  • check_circleThe intro paragraph, the eyebrow and the section heading came out. The title, a small last-updated date and the table say enough.

smartphoneSmall screens

Still usable on a phone

Sixteen columns won't fit on a phone, so the table scrolls sideways there, snapping gently to column edges, while the resin name stays pinned on the left. That way you always know which row you're reading.

insightsWhat it showed

The answers were in the spec sheets all along

  • check_circleABS-Like Resin 3.0 is the best value. It's the cheapest bottle at $25.48 and lands mid-pack or better on almost every property.
  • check_circleTough Resin 2.0 takes a hit best, at 245 J/m of impact strength, about double most of the lineup. It's also one of the priciest per kilo.
  • check_circleTough Resin Ultra bends the furthest, at 70–80% elongation, but it's the softest and weakest in tension. It's a resin for clips and hinges, not structural parts.
  • check_circleStandard Resin is among the hardest and strongest in tension, but it's also one of the most brittle.

auto_awesomeProcess

Researched, designed and shipped in a day

I built the Resin Matrix with Claude over one day. Claude gathered the specs from all 17 product pages, read the store's styles, wrote the page and published it to its own GitHub repository. I set the direction and reviewed every change on the live page.

Because each change went live within a couple of minutes, the iteration stayed quick. I could ask for something, judge it on the real page and keep it or cut it. That made it easy to take things out once I saw they weren't helping.

flagWhat's next

Things to improve

  • radio_button_uncheckedKeep the data current. Prices and specs are a snapshot from one day, and Anycubic changes prices often. A small script that re-reads the product pages on a schedule would keep the table fresh without manual edits.
  • radio_button_uncheckedCompare a shortlist side by side. Sorting finds candidates, but there's no way to pin two or three resins and see only those. A pick-and-compare mode would finish the job.
  • radio_button_uncheckedA better phone layout. Sideways scrolling works, but only three columns are visible at once. Cards that show a resin's key numbers, or a picker for which columns to show, would suit a phone better.
  • radio_button_uncheckedBeyond one brand. Every number comes from Anycubic's own spec sheets. Adding other brands, along with my own print results, would turn it into a real buyer's guide.
  • radio_button_uncheckedThe brand typeface. MiSans only loads on Anycubic's own site, so most visitors see Inter. Hosting the font files with the page would match the store exactly.
