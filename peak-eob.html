Peak Interactive EOB

An Explanation of Benefits is the statement most people open, panic over and call about. I designed an interactive version for OSG that leads with what you owe in plain language, then lets you question any claim without leaving the page.

Not a bill, said first

The welcome block says "This is NOT a bill" under the member's name, and the first callout restates the whole statement in one sentence: total charges, what insurance paid, and your share. A split bar shows "Not your responsibility" against "Your responsibility" so the answer is visible before any table.

Progress you can read at a glance

The Account Summary keeps the line items short, then moves the numbers people actually ask about into three trackers.

  • check_circleBenefit period: time remaining in the plan year.
  • check_circleDeductible: paid to date against the plan total.
  • check_circleOut-of-pocket maximum: a donut with amounts in the legend.
  • check_circleEach tracker has a plain sentence beside it explaining what happens when you hit the limit.

Claims, grouped the way people think

The Claim Breakdown table has ten money columns, so I grouped them under four plain headers: Claim Detail, What Your Provider Can Charge You, Total Claim Cost and Your Responsibility. "Your Responsibility" is bold in every row, and each claim has a "View" link for its explanation and remark codes.

Questioning a charge in two clicks

Instead of sending people to a phone line, each claim row has a checkbox. Check the claims you're unsure about, press Make Inquiry, and a dialog opens with just those rows and a comment box. The member never has to copy a claim number.

An assistant on every page

OSG's Orion AI assistant sits behind an "Ask a Question" callout on every page, not hidden in a corner. It opens with four suggested questions taken from the most common EOB calls, like "Why was my claim denied?" and "How can I dispute an error?", so members don't have to know what to ask. The FAQ answers point back to it.

A person, and access, when needed

Three header icons stay on every page: the AI assistant, accessibility and contact. The contact dialog offers a call back or a live chat in one place, and the accessibility menu (UserWay) gives contrast, text size, spacing and a dyslexia-friendly font.

The same statement, on paper

Many members still print their EOB, and some need it for records. The Print Version reflows the same content onto one letter-size page: member details, the plain-language summary, the claims table and the three trackers, with Print and Download PDF in the top bar.

Built as a kit, and what I cut

Every page is built from the same components: header, page header with member details, callouts, link cards, ads and a footer, so OSG could rebrand the template for other clients. I also explored a Family Distribution chart and an important-messages panel, and left both out to keep the first version focused on one member's bill. A one-tap survey at the bottom asks whether the statement was clear.
