Kids Week

Three kids, two schools, clubs, sports and family helping with pickups. I designed and shipped a private weekly schedule that shows at a glance where each kid needs to be, and when.

reportThe problem

The week lived in a dozen places

Our family calendar had everything, but it answers "what's at 3 PM," not "who do I pick up today, and where." School letters, club pages, the swim schedule and two school calendars added to the pile. The people who help with pickups needed one link they could read on a phone in five seconds.

view_weekThe core pattern

Days across, kids down

Each child gets a row and each day a column, with school times in a quiet gray strip and activities tinted in that child's color.

  • check_circleIt opens on today. Arrows move a full screen of days, and Today jumps back.
  • check_circleColumns resize so only whole days fit, with no sliver of a third day.
  • check_circleSwipes and drags snap to the next whole day, and a quick flick carries further.
  • check_circleLight and dark mode follow sunrise and sunset with a short fade, and respect reduced motion.

content_cutIteration

Most of the edits were deletions

Version one had everything, so I reviewed it on my phone the way the family would and removed things one at a time.

  • check_circleThe color key went first, since names beside each row already said it.
  • check_circleThe wide name column became a thin strip with names set sideways in color.
  • check_circle"Week of" left the title, and the date range moved between the arrows.
  • check_circleWalking icons briefly replaced Drop-off and Pickup, read as decoration, and the words came back.

tuneDetails

Small rules that match real life

The grid follows the rules the family already lives by.

  • check_circlePickup moves to when the later club ends, and clubs skip no-school days.
  • check_circleA toggle hides school times for an activities-only view, remembered per device.
  • check_circleActivities get an emoji from their name, and past days fade back.
  • check_circleTop-bar buttons open delayed-opening times and school contacts that dial the right extension.

celebrationSpecial days

Special days tint the whole column

Spirit Week arrived in week one. A banner cost a full row for one word, so those days became a light school-blue tint with a small label, like Today, and each child's outfit sits on a dashed card. No-school days use amber. Red read too close to one child's color, and yellow outfit cards competed with Today, so both changed.

lockPrivacy

A shareable link that keeps the schedule private

Free hosting is public, so the page protects itself. The readable schedule stays in a private repository, and only an AES-256 encrypted copy is published, with the key derived from a family passphrase. The public files hold no names, schools or times. Family members type the passphrase once, and their phone remembers it.

syncData

Built from the family calendar, not a spreadsheet

A small script now builds the schedule from our family Google Calendar and the school's club calendar.

  • check_circleA rules file turns long event titles into short names and places.
  • check_circleSchool days come from drop-off events, so deleting one makes a day off.
  • check_circleClub meetings appear only on school days and push pickup later.
  • check_circleHome-screen copies check a build stamp and reload themselves, still unlocked.

auto_awesomeProcess

Designed in conversation, shipped the same day

I built the first version with Claude in one afternoon and evening, and I refine it the same way. I make the design calls and review every change on a real phone. Claude reads the calendars, writes the code and publishes it. Seeing a change live within minutes made trying and reverting cheap, which made the round of deletions practical.
