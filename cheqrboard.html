CheQRboard

CheQRboard lets zoos, museums and venues decide what happens after someone scans a QR code. I redesigned the UI and design system for the admin platform and the mobile screens visitors land on.

eventYear

2025 · Figma

reportThe problem

A scan that went nowhere interesting

The old visitor screen was a stock welcome graphic, a language menu and a name field, the same at every venue. Staff couldn't change it without a developer. The goal was to let a zoo educator or museum coordinator build an on-brand experience, attach it to a code and schedule it on their own.

view_quiltThe core pattern

Every editor shares one layout

Collapsible sections sit on the left (Main Image, Logo, Content, Design, Results) and a phone preview on the right updates as you type. Staff learn the pattern once, and the preview always answers their real question: what will a visitor see?

categoryContent system

Eight content types, one library

Content falls into eight types, each with its own icon, and the template picker groups them by goal: interactive, navigation and multimedia. Everything lives in one library, so a single poll or video can sit behind many codes and be updated in one place.

qr_code_2QR design

Custom codes that still scan

Venues wanted codes that matched their signage, so the editor exposes body shape, eye frames, colors, a text frame and three saved presets. More freedom means more ways to break a code, so when colors hurt contrast the product flags it in place and asks again at save.

eventScheduling

One code, different content by date

A sign stays up for months but programs change weekly. Each code keeps its primary content and can swap in scheduled content, such as a Thursday evening exhibit, for set dates and times, chosen from the library in a side drawer.

tuneDesign system

The states nobody asks for

Most of the system work was making each component hold up in its empty, filled, error and edge-case states.

  • check_circlePreset colors that venues can edit, reset and reuse across every editor
  • check_circleAn image uploader with zoom, rotate and crop for logos and main images
  • check_circleA collapsible sidebar so the editor and preview fit a laptop
  • check_circleA warning before switching organizations with unsaved edits

auto_awesomeAI assistant

Rusty drafts, staff decide

Writing a good question and four good answers is hard for a busy coordinator. An assistant called Rusty lives in the header and suggests copy in context, and long descriptions get a one-click Shorten. A "Coming soon" state let the entry point ship before the feature did.

handshakeHandoff

Design QA against the real build

I checked development builds against the Figma frames with Pixelay overlays, so spacing and alignment slips were caught before release rather than after.

flagWhat's next

Things to improve

  • radio_button_uncheckedContrast checks while picking. The scan warning appears after colors are chosen. A live contrast meter beside the pickers would steer people away from unscannable codes sooner.
  • radio_button_uncheckedFewer look-alike templates. Call to Action and Image + Text use nearly the same editor and description, and could merge into one template with options.
  • radio_button_uncheckedEditing away from a desk. The collapsed rail helps on laptops, but staff standing at an exhibit with a tablet or phone still can't edit comfortably.
  • radio_button_uncheckedScans with context. The dashboard shows total scans per code. Trends over time and by placement would tell venues which signs actually work.
